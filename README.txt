AGES OF DUST
A civilisation simulation. Double-click index.html to play (Chrome or Edge work best).
Nothing to install. Saves stay inside your browser.

Random worlds with rivers, climate, ore and wild crops. Individual people, each with their
own traits, who walk real paths to work, eat, marry, fight and die.

Inventions come from what each town's people actually do and need, and belong to that town
and realm. They spread within a realm along its roads, and between realms only by trade,
if the owner is willing and the buyer can pay. From the Iron Age on scholars write to each other, so a realm's
whole effort counts towards each town's ideas (more with paper, printing and the telegraph, and printed books cross borders).
Big cities keep clerks and tutors, build further out when the middle is full, and pull down old houses for a college.
War is fought by real soldiers carrying what
their town forged. Ships are built at real ports. Cities hold only as many people as their
water, sewers, transport and towers allow; past that come disease, fires and riots.

Hunters stalk real herds, bring animals down and butcher them where they fall. Wolves, bears
and lions hunt back. Planters put in berries, trees, gardens, orchards, herbs, flax and vines.

War: every war has a goal and is ended by a treaty. Armies march as columns, need feeding,
catch fever, freeze and desert. Soldiers fight in companies under a general with a plan, and
each one has their own weapon, armour, stamina, nerve and wounds. Battles can end in a rout,
with prisoners taken. Sieges have walls, gates, engines, hunger and storming. Towns taken stay
occupied until the peace, and can rise up. Click a battle's plate, then "Watch this up close".

Water, land and getting about: seas and lakes drain downhill through named rivers; people need
fresh water. Coal, iron, oil and the rest lie in veins that fit their geology, and give each realm a
character. Snow has real depth, glaciers and avalanches, and lakes freeze. Trails become roads that
widen. People go round buildings and spread out when streets fill. Ships stay on water, rivers and
canals; towns dig canals where they pay. The tree of inventions now runs to the Stellar Age.

Money: barter, then goods as money, coin, banknotes, paper and electronic money, one currency per
realm. Towns have their own prices and wages; merchants trade where prices differ. Taxes, tariffs and
tolls fill a treasury that pays soldiers, builders, upkeep and interest. Shortfalls mean bonds, clipped
coin or printing, and so inflation, bank runs, stock crashes, default and depression. The Economy
button compares every realm. Nothing is built on water and no path crosses it; a crowded rich town
can pay heavily to fill in its shallows, but never a channel, harbour front or shipping lane.

Spreading out: when every field within a day's walk is taken, or a town goes hungry, young families
leave to found villages, choosing fresh water first, then soil, woods, game, ore their realm lacks and
a city nearby, and avoiding cold, steep and high ground. A realm that can write runs state granaries.
Paths bend round hills; nobody builds on a cliff.

Weather: real prevailing winds, drifting storms and highs, rain shadows behind mountains, seasons
opposite either side of the equator. A season's rain sets the harvest; droughts, floods, hurricanes,
mud and wildfires come out of it. See the Weather map view.

Medicine, food, transport, modern life and modern war each have a long line of inventions
(Inventions > Lines of invention). Healers, hospitals and quarantine stations save lives and slow
epidemics.

The Agricultural and Industrial Revolutions: each realm goes through its own, stage by stage (rotations,
breeds, enclosure, drills, machines; then cottage industry, mills, steam, coal and iron, factories,
railways). They lift harvests and output, empty the villages into factory towns, and bring riots and
disease until sanitation and unions catch up. Statistics shows who leads in inventions and in each one.

Food: hungry towns slaughter herds, eat famine foods, buy grain and get relief; new villages live off
the land until their first harvest. Dozens of food inventions and buildings strengthen each step.

City walls follow each town's shape (palisade, stone, bastioned), with towers that shoot during
sieges, gatehouses on the roads, repairs, wider circuits as the town grows, and a boulevard when
they come down. Only border towns with an enemy near (or a capital) build them; neighbours' rings
stop halfway and join instead of crossing, and towns deep inside a realm let theirs go in peacetime. Hurricanes are rarer but flood the low coast where they land. Map names: the Names
button (or N) cycles all names, realm names only, or none. The panels scale with the window.

Land and work: every plot has a price, dearest in a big town's centre and by the sea or a lake, cheaper out among
the farms (see the Land value map view). Shops, offices, colleges and flats take the dear land; farms, quarries and
smoky works move out to where it's cheap, and farms at the edge of a growing city are sold off as building land. Old
houses and outdated workshops on valuable ground are bought, pulled down and rebuilt as something modern.
Each kind of work splits into some 180 real trades (peasant, handloom weaver, scribe, lamplighter, docker, typist,
programmer, drone operator...) that appear and vanish with inventions and buildings. Trades need the right
schooling (apprenticed, schooled, college), pay more when short of people, and when a new machine arrives the old
trade's people are out of work until they find something else. Towns show unemployment, schooling, the main trades
and their pay, and what they're short of; people show their trade, schooling and pay.

Over a thousand inventions: hundreds of real techniques, machines and institutions in long chains across every age.

Airports and airfields with real flights between them (props, then jets and widebodies), taxiing, holding and one
plane per runway at a time. Bulk carriers, supertankers and giant container ships carry huge slow loads between big
deep-water ports; they crawl in shallow water and can't use rivers or canals. Ships, planes, trains and road traffic
keep their distance instead of passing through each other. Busy shipping lanes widen into two-way fairways where
ships keep stopping for each other; in a wide fairway each ship keeps to its own side.

Harbours: every port has berths (how many ships fit alongside at once) and a size class (small boats, sailing ships,
big ocean ships, the giant hulls). Ships queue at anchor for a free berth, are warped in, and lie alongside while dock
gangs unload and load them: porters over a gangway, then cranes, then container gantries. The harbour entrance lets
only so many ships through a year. A ship too big for every berth is worked slowly by lighters at anchor, and a port
that keeps turning ships away or keeping them waiting is rebuilt bigger in place, or the realm builds another quay or a
port down the coast, depending on how it is governed. Waterfront houses are bought up when there is no room on the
shore. 59 new kinds of port from the beach landing to the deep-water megaport (grain quays, coal staithes, oil jetties,
ferry and cruise terminals, naval dockyards, marinas...), several up to 4x4, and 13 new ships across the ages (reed
boat, dhow, junk, fluyt, paddle steamer, reefer, hovercraft, ro-ro, rotor-sail freighter, collier, gas carrier...).
Shipping costs crew, fuel, dues and handling, so a short hop to a town down the road goes by cart or lorry instead,
and an empty run isn't worth sailing. Small boats are real: boatwrights build them, towns buy them and have them
carted or rowed over, keep them in boathouses, and fishers take one out and bring it back.

Offices: white-collar work needs desks, from a sliver of the economy in the Middle Ages to a third of all work in the
information age. Towns build offices of the sector that is short (business, trading houses and shipping, government,
law, banking and insurance, press and media, technology), sized to the gap and in the fashion of their age: 100 kinds
from the scribes' house and counting house to bank chambers, ministries, art deco towers, glass curtain walls, business
parks, tech campuses and supertall towers. Short of desks, trade and research slow; spare desks stand empty and rents
fall. Offices create real jobs (clerks, secretaries, civil servants, underwriters, call-centre workers, analysts...).
When the middle of town is full, offices, banks and flats buy out old houses there and pay the families.
Mid-rise blocks: a crowded town that couldn't fill a tower builds four- to eight-storey blocks instead, in 100 styles
from the Renaissance palazzo and Georgian terrace to Haussmann, art deco, panel blocks and solarpunk courtyards.
Map options: "Far buildings" turns off the little building squares when zoomed out; realm names shrink as you zoom
out, small realms' names are hidden, and names never overlap.

Speed: walking that happens off screen is done without route searches or steps (the same time,
the same worn trails), so big worlds run about twice as fast.

The modern world: telephones, microchips, mobile phones, social media, online shopping, video games,
supermarkets, car plants, chip foundries, battery gigafactories and tech campuses. A World Assembly
cools grudges and shortens wars between its members; the International Games come every four years;
tourists fly in to see wonders, beaches and theme parks and spend their money. Nuclear realms are
rarely attacked. In war: assault rifles, ceramic body armour, rocket launchers, jet fighters, combat
drones, helicopter gunships and cyber attacks that black out enemy capitals.

Money between realms: floating exchange rates, foreign loans and defaults, migrants' savings and the
money they send home, aid, tribute and reparations. War has a home front: support at home rises and
falls with the war, and rich realms hire mercenaries.

Earth: pick "Earth" under Geography to play on the real continents. The seed still decides the fine
coastline, hills, rain, rivers, soil, ore and wild crops.

Also: earthquakes, tsunamis, volcanoes, wildfires, locusts and great storms; faiths that spread, split
and persecute (Faith map view); diet, literacy, smog and wealth change how long people live; slopes
erode, old buildings fall down, and families pass on names and money.

A million people: past what your computer can run, towns keep a sample of real individuals and
treat the rest as crowds that work, grow and die at the same rates.

Controls
  Drag / WASD   move the map        Scroll      zoom
  Click         inspect anything    Double-click a person: focus on them (F)
  Space         pause               1-5         speed
  M             map view            B           realm colours
  T             inventions          H           hide all panels
  Esc           close / cancel      The - on any panel minimises it
  Pick or hover a realm in the list to see its towns linked to its capital on the map.

Tools (bottom of the screen)
  Fate, Land, Resources, Soil. Click or drag on the map.
  "times" repeats a power with one click. Keep leaves the tool selected.

tools/headless.js runs the simulation without graphics for balance testing:
  node tools/headless.js [seed] [years] [width] [height] [continents|pangea|archipelago|inland|earth]
  Set MAXPOP to change the population limit, QUIET=1 for one summary line, LOG=1 for the chronicle.
